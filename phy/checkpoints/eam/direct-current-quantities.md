---
title: The four quantities
---

::: exercise charge-q1
A coulomb is the combined charge of roughly how many electrons?

::: answer
About $6 \times 10^{18}$. The coulomb is a pile of electron charges large enough to be a useful
unit.
:::
:::

::: exercise current-q1
A current of 2 A flows for 5 minutes. How much charge passes a given point?

::: answer
$Q = I t = 2 \times 300 = 600\ \text{C}$. Convert the 5 minutes to 300 seconds first.
:::
:::

::: exercise voltage-q1
How much energy does a 12 V battery give to each coulomb of charge that passes through it?

::: answer
12 joules. One volt is one joule per coulomb, so 12 V gives each coulomb 12 J.
:::
:::

::: exercise resistance-q1
Two wires are the same material and length, but one is thicker. Which has the lower resistance?

::: answer
The thicker one. A wider cross-section lets the electrons through more easily, so resistance
falls as the wire gets fatter.
:::
:::

::: exercise ohms-law-q1
A 9 V supply is connected across a 3 Ω resistor. What current flows?

::: answer
$I = V / R = 9 / 3 = 3\ \text{A}$. Divide the voltage by the resistance.
:::
:::

::: exercise ohms-law-q2
A component carries 0.5 A when 6 V sits across it. What is its resistance?

::: answer
$R = V / I = 6 / 0.5 = 12\ \Omega$. Divide the voltage by the current.
:::
:::
