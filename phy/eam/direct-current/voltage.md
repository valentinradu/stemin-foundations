---
title: Voltage
---

::: card
The charge is already in the wire. The source does not add electrons; it creates a pressure
difference between the two ends of the conductor, and that pressure gets the existing electrons
moving the same way. We call that pressure **voltage**, write it $V$, and measure it in
**volts**.
:::

::: card
Voltage is always a difference between two points, never a property of one point alone. A 12 V
battery means 12 volts between its terminals. You measure it across a component, because "the
voltage at one point" is as meaningless as "the height of a place" without saying height above
what.
:::

::: card
The volt is defined by energy. One volt means one joule is exchanged per coulomb of charge moved
between the two points. A 12 V battery gives each coulomb 12 joules; a 230 V socket gives each
coulomb 230 joules. That is the energy each coulomb picks up from the source and will spend in
the circuit.
:::

::: card
A phone charger runs at 5 V, a car battery at 12 V, household mains at 230 V in Europe. What
harms you is not voltage directly but the current it drives through your body. Your skin's
resistance limits the current at 12 V to a harmless fraction of a milliamp; at 230 V the same
resistance allows enough to be fatal.
:::

::: exercise q1
How much energy does a 12 V battery give to each coulomb of charge that passes through it?

::: answer
12 joules. One volt is one joule per coulomb, so 12 V gives each coulomb 12 J.
:::
:::
