---
title: Charge
---

::: card
Direct current flows steadily in one direction, the kind a battery, a solar panel, or a phone
charger delivers. Five quantities carry the whole of it: charge, current, voltage, resistance,
and power. One law ties the first four together. They all begin with what moves inside the wire.
:::

::: card
In an insulator like rubber or plastic, each electron is locked in a bond between two atoms and
goes nowhere. In a metal, the outer electrons belong to no single atom; they drift freely
through the whole lattice. So a metal wire is packed with electrons wandering every which way,
going nowhere in particular, because nothing makes them all go the same way.
:::

::: card
Each electron carries a fixed amount of **charge**, the property that makes particles push and
pull on each other. Charge is a property of the electron, the way mass is a property of an
object. We write it $Q$ and measure it in **coulombs**.
:::

::: card
One coulomb is the combined charge of about $6 \times 10^{18}$ electrons. A single electron's
charge is far too small to work with, so the coulomb is just a pile big enough to be useful.
:::

::: exercise q1
A coulomb is the combined charge of roughly how many electrons?

::: answer
About $6 \times 10^{18}$. The coulomb is a pile of electron charges large enough to be a useful
unit.
:::
:::
