---
title: The one weakness of DC
---

::: card
DC electrons drift slowly in one direction, always. That steady drift carries metal ions with
it. An ion that moves leaves a gap where it was and deposits where it arrives. The end where
current leaves the metal into the surrounding soil or water corrodes. On a grid carrying DC for
decades, this eats at every buried conductor.
:::

::: card
Alternating current reverses direction fifty times a second in Europe. The electrons jiggle back
and forth instead of marching. Ions migrate one way for half a cycle, then back for the next, so
the net migration over a full cycle is nearly zero. The corrosion nearly cancels.
:::

::: card
The electrons in a house wire sway by about the width of a bacterium, yet full kilowatts arrive
instantly. The energy is not carried by the electrons; it travels in the electric and magnetic
fields around the conductor, near the speed of light. The electrons are the medium, not the
messenger, the way air carries sound without itself travelling from speaker to ear.
:::

::: card
The cancellation of corrosion is one reason the world's grids run on alternating current. The
rest of the story comes in a later part.
:::

::: exercise q1
Why does alternating current cause far less electrolytic corrosion than direct current?

::: answer
Because the current reverses each half cycle. Ions migrate one way, then back, so the net
migration over a full cycle is nearly zero and the corrosion cancels.
:::
:::
