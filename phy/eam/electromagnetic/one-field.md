---
title: One field, two faces
---

::: card
A stationary charge creates only an electric field. Move past that same charge and it is no
longer stationary from your point of view: a moving charge is a current, which means a magnetic
field. Same charge, different observer, different fields measured.
:::

::: card
Einstein showed in 1905 that this is no coincidence. Electric and magnetic fields are two aspects
of one underlying thing: the electromagnetic field. What one observer measures as purely
electric, another measures as a mixture. They are one field, seen from different angles in
spacetime.
:::

::: card
This is why $\varepsilon_0$ and $\mu_0$ are related through $c$: not two independent constants
but two faces of one, tied by $c^2 = 1/(\varepsilon_0 \mu_0)$. Light is what the electromagnetic
field looks like when its two faces sustain each other through empty space.
:::

::: exercise q1
Observer A stands still beside a current-carrying wire and measures a magnetic field. Observer B
moves alongside the electrons and measures an electric field. Which observer is correct?

::: answer
Both. Electric and magnetic fields are aspects of one electromagnetic field; which components
appear depends on the observer's motion relative to the source. Neither measurement is more real
than the other.
:::
:::
