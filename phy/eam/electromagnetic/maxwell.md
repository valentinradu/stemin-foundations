---
title: The symmetry Maxwell completed
---

::: card
Capacitors and inductors showed that fields store energy and resist change. Now a deeper
question: what are they, really? The answer runs through Maxwell's equations to light, and then
to Einstein's discovery that the two fields are not separate things at all.
:::

::: card
Faraday found one direction experimentally: a changing magnetic field creates an electric field.
Spin a coil in a magnetic field and a voltage appears. Measured, proven, and used in every
generator ever built.
:::

::: card
Maxwell asked whether the reverse held: does a changing electric field create a magnetic field?
There was no experimental evidence. But without that term his equations were inconsistent,
violating conservation of charge in some situations. He added the missing piece on theoretical
grounds alone.
:::

::: card
The result was a perfect symmetry. A changing electric field creates a magnetic field, Maxwell's
addition to Ampère's law. A changing magnetic field creates an electric field, Faraday's law.
Each kind of changing field calls the other into being.
:::

::: exercise q1
Maxwell added his extra term with no experimental evidence for it. What forced him to add it?

::: answer
Consistency. Without it his equations violated conservation of charge in some situations, so he
added the term on theoretical grounds alone. Experiment confirmed it later.
:::
:::
