---
title: What a field is
---

::: card
Rub a balloon on your hair, then hold it just above some small scraps of paper. The scraps jump
up to meet it before it touches them. Something reaches across the gap and pulls them.
:::

::: card
That something is a field. The balloon changes the space around it: at every point nearby, a
definite pull is now present, waiting for anything that can feel it. Put a scrap at one of those
points and it feels exactly that pull. The field is this influence spread through space, there at
every point even when nothing is present to respond.
:::

::: card
The most familiar field is gravity. The Earth fills the space around it with a gravitational
field, and any mass placed in that field feels a pull. The Moon feels it across 380,000 km of
empty space, which is what holds it in orbit ([Figure](figure:gravity-field)).
:::

::: figure gravity-field
![Earth's field reaching the Moon](assets/gravity-field.svg)

Earth's gravitational field reaches every point of the surrounding space, drawing the distant
Moon inward across the emptiness.
:::

::: card
An electric field works the same way, for charge instead of mass. Any charged object fills the
space around it with an electric field. Place another charge in that field and it feels a force:
opposite charges attract, like charges repel. The rubbed balloon was charged; the paper felt its
field.
:::

::: exercise q1
A field acts on objects without touching them. What property must an object have to feel a force
in an electric field?

::: answer
Charge. An electric field acts on charge, the way a gravitational field acts on mass.
:::
:::
