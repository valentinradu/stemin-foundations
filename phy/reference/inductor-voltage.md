---
title: Inductor voltage
---

::: equation
V = L \frac{di}{dt}
:::

::: legend
$V$: voltage across the inductor, in volts
$L$: inductance, in henries
$\frac{di}{dt}$: rate of change of current, in amperes per second
:::

The voltage across an inductor is its inductance times the rate of change of the current through
it.

A steady current gives zero voltage: the inductor is just a wire. A changing current gives an
opposing voltage proportional to how fast it changes. A henry is one volt per ampere per second.
